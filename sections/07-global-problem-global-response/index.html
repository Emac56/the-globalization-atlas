Global issue

Plastic Pollution in the Philippines’ Rivers and Oceans

Six parts

Select a part to explore the problem and the global response.

Part 01 / 06

What is the problem?

Answer

Plastic pollution happens when plastic waste enters rivers, coastlines, and oceans. It threatens marine life and the environment.

Part 02 / 06

Why can't one country solve it alone?

Answer

Plastic waste can cross borders through rivers, ocean currents, and global trade. Countries need to work together to address this problem.

Part 03 / 06

Who is involved?

Answer

Governments, businesses, communities, waste workers, and individuals all play a role. Everyone has a part in reducing plastic pollution.

Part 04 / 06

What cooperation is possible?

Answer

Countries can share technology, improve recycling, and strengthen waste management. They can also work together on international solutions.

Part 05 / 06

How is the Philippines affected?

Answer

Plastic waste threatens Philippine rivers, coastlines, and marine life. It can also affect fishing communities and tourism.

Part 06 / 06

What can individuals do?

Answer

We can use reusable bags and bottles, segregate waste, and avoid unnecessary single-use plastics. We can also join community cleanups.

Why does this problem require global cooperation?

Answer

Plastic pollution crosses borders, so one country cannot solve it alone. Global cooperation helps countries reduce waste and protect our shared oceans.
